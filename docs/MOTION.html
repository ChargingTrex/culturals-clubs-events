Culturals design system · Section 7

Motion, hue & transitions

How things move and change colour in Culturals, and how one page hands over to the next. Motion has one job here: to show where something came from and where it went. It is quick, it settles without fuss, and it never plays just to be looked at. Every example on this page is live — turn on Slow motion to study one, or Reduce motion to see what people who switch animation off get instead.

7.1Principles

Four rules decide every animation. When a new one is proposed, it has to pass all four.

1

Motion explains a change

Every animation answers “where did this come from?” or “where did it go?”. If it answers neither, cut it. Nothing moves on its own to look lively.

2

Quick and calm

Most UI motion takes 100–250 ms. Only things that cross the screen take 350 ms. Leaving is faster than arriving. Nothing waits for an animation to finish before it can be tapped.

3

Colour moves with meaning

A hue changes only when a status, an edit or a club changes — never as decoration. Colours blend through Oklab, so a change doesn't dip through a dark, greyish middle.

4

It can always be switched off

With Reduce motion on, slides and springs become 150 ms crossfades and loops stop. The meaning stays: the colour, icon and word still change.

7.2Duration & easing

Material 3's timing and curves for structure, Cupertino springs for anything a finger touches. Durations are tokens — never type a number of milliseconds into a component.

7.3Hue & colour change

Colour is the quietest motion we have and the one that carries the most meaning. These rules say how strong a hue gets, how it blends from one to another, and when it is allowed to flash.

Wash strengths

Every colour role is used at five strengths. The four washes are the role's colour mixed into the surface, so the same token works in light and dark. 8 % hover · 12 % selected or a settled edit · 16 % dragging · 24 % the peak of a flash, which always fades back · solid for fills with an on- colour on top.

Tokens · Hue

One ramp per colour role

Status hues for status, edit hues for edits, club accents for clubs — the ramp never borrows a colour for a different job.

--wash-hover 8%--wash-selected 12%--wash-drag 16%--wash-flash 24%color-mix(in oklab, role N%, surface)
Hue · State layers

Hover and press add a layer, they don't swap the colour

A layer of the content colour at 8 % (hover) or 12 % (pressed) sits over the surface. It goes in over 100 ms and out over 150 ms. Because it is the content colour, it reads the same on a blue button, a tonal button and a white row, in both themes.

--state-hover .08--state-pressed .12in 100ms · out 150ms
Hue · Blending

Blend in Oklab, not sRGB

Browsers blend hex colours in sRGB, where the middle of a blend comes out darker and greyer than either end — clearest on red to green. Oklab keeps the middle as light as the ends. The chips underneath blend “Pending” to “Approved” both ways — watch the middle.

@property --mixcolor-mix(in oklab, to var(--mix), from)150ms standard

When a hue is allowed to flash

A flash is a 24 % wash that fades back to rest. It marks something that changed while the person was looking — and only that. It happens once, never loops, and never more than three times a second.

7.5Transitions between pages

How one page hands over to the next depends on how the two are related. Peers fade, children slide in from the side, a card can grow into its own page, and anything temporary rises from the bottom. The nav bar and the top bar's row never move — they are where the eye rests while everything else changes.

Try it

Use the phone like the app — tap the nav bar, an event, the Up next card, My QR — or pick a transition below.

A tap during a transition is never ignored: the running one jumps to its end and the new one starts at once. Nobody waits for an animation.

What stays still

The nav bar

It belongs to the app, not the page, so it never fades or slides with page changes. Only its indicator moves.

The top bar's row

The back button and actions stay put on tab switches; on a push, the old title slides out as the new one slides in, as on iOS.

The club's hue

When a club's card opens into a page, its accent goes with it — the band on the card becomes the tint behind the page's header, so the colour connects the two.

Focus

After any page transition, keyboard and screen-reader focus moves to the new page's title. After a sheet closes, it returns to the button that opened it.

7.6Reduce motion

When the device asks for less motion (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce), every movement becomes a short crossfade and every loop stops. Colour, icons and words still change, so nothing is lost but the travel. Turn on Reduce motion at the top and replay anything on this page.

7.7Timing reference

Every animation in the app on one table. Animate transform, opacity, clip-path and colour. A background position is fine for small fills like the skeleton. Never animate width, height, top or margin — they make the browser redo the layout every frame and stutter on phones.

WhatTriggerPropertiesDurationEasing
The scan line in components.css still moves with top — switch it to translateY. The segmented thumb, tab indicator and day pill in these demos transition width, which is acceptable for something this small; use scaleX when they are built into the components.

7.8Building it

Copy the tokens into tokens.css next to the colour tokens. The web app is made of separate HTML pages, so page transitions use cross-document View Transitions: browsers that support them animate, the rest just load the next page.

Page transitions in the web app

Each student page gets the same stylesheet and one small script in its <head>. The script has to be a classic script, not a module: it must run before the new page's first frame to tell the browser which kind of transition this is.

Colour blends in Oklab

Do and don't

Do

  • Use the tokens; one change to a token retimes the whole app.
  • Let people tap through an animation — start the next one from where the last one is.
  • Keep loops for “live now” only, and stop them when the state ends.
  • Move focus to the new page's title after a transition.
  • Test every new animation with Reduce motion on.

Don't

  • Animate on page load just to be noticed, or bounce anything but the seal and a new count.
  • Slide between peers in the nav bar — they have no left or right.
  • Flash a whole screen, or flash anything more than three times a second.
  • Fade colours through sRGB, or change a hue to decorate.
  • Animate certificates in print or PDF.
